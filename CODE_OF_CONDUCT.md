# Code of Conduct

This repository is a project of the Deoband Community Wikimedia user group, and it
follows the [Universal Code of Conduct](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Universal_Code_of_Conduct),
which applies across all Wikimedia projects and spaces. Contributors who are used to
Wikimedia's technical spaces will also recognise the
[Code of Conduct for Wikimedia technical spaces](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Code_of_Conduct),
and we hold ourselves to the same standard here.

Rather than restate either document, this file covers what those standards mean day to
day in this repository, and who to talk to when something goes wrong.

## What we expect

Contributors here come from different countries, speak different first languages, and
have wildly different amounts of programming experience. Some are writing their first
pull request. The whole thing works because people are patient with each other.

In practice that means:

- Assume good faith. A blunt-sounding comment is usually someone writing in their
  second or third language, not someone being rude.
- Review the code, not the person. "This query isn't parameterised" is useful.
  "Did you even read the docs" is not.
- Be generous with context. If you know why something is done a certain way, say so
  instead of just saying no.
- Accept that maintainers say no sometimes, and that they owe you a reason when
  they do.
- Ask questions freely. A beginner's question costs a few minutes; a beginner who was
  made to feel stupid and left costs us a contributor.

## What isn't acceptable

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained hostility toward anyone.
- Insults or attacks based on religion, sect, caste, ethnicity, nationality, language,
  gender, sexual orientation, disability, age, or appearance.
- Sexualised language, imagery, or unwanted attention of any kind.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their clear permission, including
  real names, addresses, workplaces, or private messages.
- Deliberately derailing discussions, or repeatedly pushing a point after a decision
  has been made and explained.
- Sneaking malicious code, backdoors, or data exfiltration into a contribution. Given
  what this portal handles, we treat this as the most serious thing on the list.

## Where this applies

Everywhere this project lives: issues, pull requests, code review comments, commit
messages, discussions, and the project's spaces on other platforms. It also applies
when you're representing the project somewhere else, such as at a Wikimedia event or
in an online space where you're speaking as a contributor here.

## Reporting a problem

If someone's behaviour in this repository is making it hard for you to take part,
contact the maintainers privately rather than replying in the thread. You can reach
them through the contact details on the [Deoband Community Wikimedia](https://dcwwiki.org/)
site, or by direct message on GitHub.

Tell us what happened and roughly when, with links if you have them. You don't need to
prove anything or write it up formally.

We'll keep your report confidential, we won't share your identity with the person you're
reporting without asking you first, and we won't retaliate or allow retaliation against
anyone for raising something honestly. Reports involving a maintainer are taken just as
seriously as any other, and the maintainer concerned won't be part of handling it.

If a matter concerns Wikimedia conduct more broadly, or you'd rather not go through the
maintainers of this repository at all, the Universal Code of Conduct has its own
reporting and enforcement routes, described in the
[UCoC Enforcement Guidelines](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Universal_Code_of_Conduct/Enforcement_guidelines).

## What we'll do about it

Maintainers are responsible for acting on reports, and for removing or editing comments,
commits, issues, and pull requests that don't belong here.

We'll look into what happened, talk to the people involved, and respond in proportion.
Depending on how serious it is and whether it's a pattern, that might mean a private
word, a public correction, having a comment removed, a temporary block from the
repository, or a permanent one. We'll tell you what we decided.

Most situations are a misunderstanding that a short private conversation fixes. We'd
much rather do that than ban someone.
